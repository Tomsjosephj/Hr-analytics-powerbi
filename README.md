# Hr-analytics-powerbi
I have created an interactive Power BI report that analyzes employee headcount, hiring trends, workforce demographics and attrition drivers for a company of 1,470 employees (2012 to 2022).

# Business questions

How many employees are active vs. inactive, and what is the overall attrition rate?

How has hiring developed from 2012 to 2022?

Which departments and job roles make up the workforce?

What does the workforce look like by gender, age, marital status and ethnicity?

Which factors overtime, business travel, tenure, department, job role are linked to higher attrition?

# Tools used

Power BI Desktop, DAX, Power Query, data modeling.

# Key Findings

Overall attrition is 16.1%. 237 of 1,470 employees have left, leaving 1,233 active.

Technology dominates the workforce. It accounts for 828 of 1,233 active employees (about 67%), followed by Sales with 354 (about 29%) and Human Resources with 51 (about 4%).

Headcount is concentrated in a few roles. Sales Executive (270), Software Engineer (247) and Data Scientist (199) are the three largest active roles, together about 58% of active employees.

Hiring is steady. Roughly 90 to 130 active hires per year from 2012 to 2022, with a dip around 2016 and 2017.

Marital status split: Married 42.5%, Single 37.4%, Divorced 20.2%.

# Recommendations

Reduce overtime: review workloads and staffing in teams with heavy overtime, and set overtime limits or compensation, since overtime is linked to nearly 3x higher attrition.

Strengthen the first two years: improve onboarding, assign mentors and hold regular check-ins in year 0 and year 1, when about a third of employees leave.

Review travel expectations: consider rotating or reducing travel for frequent-travel roles.

Monitor monthly: use this dashboard to track attrition by department, role and tenure so HR can act early.
